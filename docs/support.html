Support

We're happy to help. Email us at mybaby@manhattin.com and tell us what's going on.

Please don't include health details you'd rather keep private. We don't need your baby's log to help, and we can't see it.

Frequently asked questions

How do I invite my partner, and what happens on their phone?

In MyBaby, go to More → Settings → Invite partner and send the iCloud link by Messages, Mail or anything else. Sharing uses iCloud, so the app asks to turn iCloud on first.

Your partner installs MyBaby and opens the link on their iPhone. They should open the link rather than set up a new baby. If they already started an empty one, it's removed when they join. From then on you both see and add to the same log, and an entry logged on one phone shows up on the other within a few seconds when it's online. The night shift plan and the parents' names sync too, while each phone keeps its own alert settings and its own "This iPhone belongs to" (in Settings → Parents). After joining, Today asks once "Who's using this iPhone?" so your phone buzzes the right person.

To remove someone, stop sharing, or leave a log, go to Settings → iCloud & sharing → Manage sharing.

How do I turn iCloud on or off?

Go to More → Settings → iCloud & sharing → Sync with iCloud. With it off (the default), your log stays only on your iPhone. With it on, your log is backed up, end-to-end encrypted, to your own iCloud and kept in sync on your devices signed in to the same Apple Account.

iCloud can't be turned off while the log is shared. Stop sharing or leave the log in Manage sharing first. Turning sync off keeps everything on your iPhone. To also remove the copy in iCloud, use Delete my iCloud copy in the same section.

How do I restore my log on a new phone?

This works if you had iCloud sync on. Sign in to the new iPhone with the same Apple Account, install MyBaby, and tap Restore from iCloud on the welcome screen. Your log downloads from iCloud. Your iCloud Keychain needs to be available on the new phone, because it holds the keys that unlock your encrypted log.

If you'd joined a log your partner shared, open their invitation link on the new phone.

We have twins. How do I add another baby?

Tap the baby's name at the top of Today, or go to More → Babies, then Add a baby. Each baby has their own log, history, reports, health records, reminders and export, and you can switch between them the same way.

If you share with your partner, a new baby joins the same share automatically. New babies are added on the phone of the parent who created the log.

How do night shifts work, and why do alerts only vibrate?

More → Night shifts lets you split each night, or take turns by night, so both parents get equal sleep. With Family sharing, the plan syncs to your partner's phone by itself.

The parents' names, and who each iPhone belongs to, are in More → Settings → Parents. Any two parents work, including two moms or two dads. On each phone, choose its owner under "This iPhone belongs to" (or "Shared iPhone" for a phone you both use), so night-shift wake-ups and cry alerts only buzz that person. The same choice is also in Night shifts, under Reminders on this iPhone.

Wake-ups are vibrate-only by default, so the other parent and the baby keep sleeping. The buzz repeats every 30 seconds, up to 5 times, until you tap I'm up. Vibration needs haptics turned on in iPhone Settings → Sounds & Haptics. Prefer a sound? Change Alert style to Sound in Night shifts.

How do smart feed and diaper reminders work?

Turn them on in More → Settings → Feed & diaper reminders. Set Feeding reminder to Smart (learns from your log) and MyBaby looks at how far apart feeds have been over the last 5 days, counting from the start of one feed to the start of the next, with the most recent days counting most. Daytime and nighttime are learned separately (night is your night shifts' hours, or 7 PM to 7 AM). Feeds less than 40 minutes apart count as one. You get a notification a little before the next feed is likely, like "Mavie will probably be hungry around 2:40 (usually every 2 h 50 min)", and Today shows "Next feed around 2:40" under the last feed. Tap that line to see how it was worked out and which data it used.

Diaper check works the same way from your diaper changes, and if most changes come right after a feed, it expects the next one after the next feed. It's a gentle "Diaper check?" and never goes off while the baby is asleep.

It's never later than Never wait longer than, which you can set. Feed reminders still come during a sleep, since young babies may need waking; turn on Let baby sleep if you'd rather not. During a night shift only the parent on duty is reminded, with their alert style. Everything is worked out on your iPhone; nothing is sent anywhere. Ask your pediatrician how long your baby can go between feeds, especially in the first weeks.

Why is the predicted time off?

It can only learn from what's logged, and babies change fast. Common reasons:

  • Not much logged yet. With fewer than about 8 gaps between feeds in the last few days, the reminder uses your "Every few hours" time, or a common gap for your baby's age, and Today doesn't show a prediction.
  • Feeds that weren't logged. A missed feed looks like a long gap. Long stretches with nothing logged are skipped, but a single missed feed in a busy day isn't.
  • A growth spurt or cluster feeding. Patterns shift for a few days at a time. Recent days count most, so it catches up within a day or two.
  • Feeds logged late or with the wrong time. Fix the time in History and the prediction updates right away.

It's a guess from your log, not advice. Feed your baby when they seem hungry, whatever the app says.

What should I do about time I didn't log?

Mark it, so it isn't learned as a long gap. When nothing has been logged for a while, Today asks about it: tap Not tracked if you weren't logging (the baby was with grandma, at daycare, or you forgot), or Catch up to add rough counts. You can also mark any stretch from History → Mark time. Predictions skip time marked Not tracked, long stretches with nothing logged, and caught-up entries, so a forgotten night doesn't teach MyBaby that your baby sleeps 9 hours. "How MyBaby predicts this" says how many missing stretches it skipped.

Why did I get a reminder after my partner already fed the baby?

Reminders move as soon as your partner's feed reaches your iPhone through iCloud, usually within seconds, even while MyBaby is in the background. If your phone couldn't sync in time (no connection, Low Power Mode, or iOS holding back background activity), a reminder can still go off. iOS also doesn't wake an app you swiped away in the app switcher until you open it again. Opening MyBaby catches up right away. Make sure iCloud sync is on for both of you, and that Background App Refresh is allowed for MyBaby in iPhone Settings → General.

Does MyBaby work with Apple Watch?

Yes. Install MyBaby on your Apple Watch (from the Watch app on your iPhone, under Available Apps) to see the time since the last feed and diaper, log a diaper, sleep, bottle or medicine, and run the breastfeeding timer from your wrist. Add the MyBaby complication to a watch face to see the last feed at a glance. Taps on the watch are sent to your iPhone to log; if the iPhone is out of reach, they're saved and sent when it's back.

Alerts reach your wrist too. If you're wearing your Apple Watch and your iPhone is locked, iOS sends the alert to your wrist as a tap. If the watch is on its charger, your iPhone buzzes instead.

How do I use Siri with MyBaby?

Say what you want followed by “in MyBaby” (or “in My Baby”), so Siri knows which app you mean. For example: “Log a wet diaper in MyBaby”, “MyBaby, the baby's asleep”, “The baby woke up in MyBaby”, “Ask MyBaby about today”, “Log a temperature in MyBaby”, “Start cry alerts in MyBaby” or “Add a note in MyBaby”. With twins, add the name: “Log a diaper for Noah in MyBaby”.

If Siri doesn't recognize these at first, open MyBaby once, and check that Siri is set up in iPhone Settings (under Siri, or Siri & Search) and that MyBaby is allowed to use it there, in the list of apps. Starting cry alerts opens MyBaby, because iPhone only lets the microphone start while the app is on screen.

The Shortcuts app lists everything MyBaby can do, including actions without a Siri phrase, like switching breastfeeding sides or logging medicine. Open Shortcuts, tap +, then search for MyBaby. Siri never reads your diary back.

Any tips for cry alerts?

  • Start listening from Today or More → Cry alerts → Start listening.
  • Keep the phone close to the baby and plugged in.
  • Leave MyBaby running in the background. Swiping the app away stops listening.
  • If it misses cries or buzzes too often, adjust the sensitivity (Low, Medium or High).
  • Both parents' phones can listen. With a night plan, only the phone of the parent on duty buzzes, and the other phone joins in if the crying goes on.

Listening happens on your iPhone. Audio is never recorded, saved or sent. Cry alerts help you rest, but they aren't a medical or safety monitor.

Can I use an old iPhone or iPad as a baby monitor?

Yes. Install MyBaby on the spare device and sign in to iCloud with a parent's Apple Account, or with another Apple Account that you've invited to the log with permission to make changes. Turn on iCloud sync, then open More → Cry alerts → Use this device as the baby monitor. Keep it plugged in and on Wi-Fi, at least 7 feet (2 m) from the crib if you use its white noise.

  • Open MyBaby once on each parent's iPhone with iCloud on, so it can receive the monitor's alerts. Pick whose phone it is under Night shifts → This iPhone belongs to.
  • With a night plan, only the parent on duty is alerted, and the other parent joins in if the crying goes on. With no shift going on, every parent's phone is alerted.
  • Tap Test alert on the monitor to check that both phones get it.
  • Your phones show the monitor on Today, with when it last checked in, and warn you if it goes quiet.
  • Alerts arrive through Apple's iCloud, usually within seconds, but Apple doesn't guarantee how fast. They can't be marked time-sensitive, so allow MyBaby in your Sleep Focus.
  • Routing to the parent on duty needs each parent on their own Apple Account. If you share one, every phone on it gets every alert.

The monitor listens on the device; audio never leaves it. It helps you rest, but it isn't a medical or safety device.

I use a Sleep Focus or Do Not Disturb. Will I still get alerts?

Add MyBaby to the Focus's allowed apps: iPhone Settings → Focus → Sleep (or Do Not Disturb) → Apps → add MyBaby. Also make sure notifications are allowed for MyBaby in iPhone Settings → Notifications.

Why does it ask for Face ID?

Your care page (shown under your name, like "Sam's care") is private, so MyBaby locks it with Face ID, Touch ID or your iPhone's passcode, in case someone else is holding your phone. It locks again when you leave the page, put MyBaby in the background, or come back after more than 2 minutes. To turn the lock off, open your care page, tap the lock icon at the top and turn off Lock with Face ID. If your iPhone has no passcode, the page can't be locked and says so.

How do I export a report for my pediatrician?

Go to Reports, pick 7, 14 or 30 days, and tap Share PDF for a one-page report you can print, email or show at a checkup. For the full log as a spreadsheet, go to More → Settings → Export all data (CSV). With more than one baby, the export is for the baby you're viewing. Exports go only where you send them.

How do I send feedback or report a bug?

In MyBaby, go to More → Settings → Help & support → Send feedback. Pick what it's about (something's not working, an idea, something you love, or a question), write your message, and add a screenshot if it helps. MyBaby opens an email from your own account with everything filled in, so you see exactly what's sent and can change anything first. Nothing is sent from the app by itself.

You can include a few technical details, like the app and iOS versions and the iPhone model, and read the exact text first. If MyBaby crashed or froze, you can also attach the latest crash report, which stays on your iPhone until you do. If Mail isn't set up, MyBaby can start the message in another email app or copy it for you to send to mybaby@manhattin.com.

Once the code is public, you can also report bugs and suggest ideas as issues on the project's GitHub page. Issues are public, so please leave out your baby's name and health details there.

How do I delete my data?

  • Delete single entries by swiping them away in History.
  • Delete your iCloud copy in Settings → iCloud & sharing → Delete my iCloud copy. The log on your iPhone stays.
  • Delete the app to remove everything on your iPhone.
  • Manage iCloud data in iPhone Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage.
  • Stop sharing or leave a shared log in Settings → iCloud & sharing → Manage sharing.

The privacy policy has more detail.

How do I keep track of allergies, and share them with a caregiver?

Go to Health → Allergies. Tap + to add an allergy (milk, egg, peanut, tree nuts, soy, wheat, fish, shellfish, sesame and common medicines like amoxicillin and penicillin are one tap away) with its status, severity, the reactions you've seen, who diagnosed it, and whether there's an epinephrine auto-injector and where it's kept. Log a reaction records when it happened, what your baby had, the symptoms, what you did and, if you like, a photo of a rash. One tap adds the food as a suspected allergy.

If you tick trouble breathing or swelling, MyBaby shows your region's emergency number with a call button. It doesn't give any other medical advice: call your pediatrician with questions, and emergency services in an emergency.

Tag allergens when you log solids to see them in Introducing foods (when each was first and last given). MyBaby also warns you, without stopping you, when a solids log or a medicine matches something on the allergy list. For daycare, grandparents or a babysitter, open Allergy card for caregivers and share it as a PDF or as text. It goes only where you send it.

Which help lines does MyBaby show, and how do I change the country?

The support lines at the bottom of your private care screen (in More) follow your region: the emergency number first, then crisis lines, pregnancy and new-parent support, and health lines such as 811 or NHS 111, with their hours and languages when known. MyBaby takes the region from your iPhone's Region setting (iPhone Settings → General → Language & Region), not from your location, and never asks for location access.

To see another country's lines, tap Change next to "Support for …" and pick it from the list, or choose Other / not listed for a reminder to call your local emergency number and a link to Find A Helpline, an international directory. In Canada, choose your province or territory to see its own lines, like Info-Santé and Info-Social 811 and 1 866 APPELLE in Quebec.

Every number was checked on the organization's own website or a government health site, and the app shows when. If one doesn't work, call your local emergency number, and please let us know so we can fix it.
